What it is

The shape of the system

The premise is that a locating business is three separate machines that happen to share a phone. Leads arrive and have to be sorted. Sorted leads have to be answered, in the operator's own voice, without breaking fair-housing law. Answering requires knowing who actually works at a property this month — which almost nobody knows, because the industry churns.

Each of those is built as its own container with its own tests, able to run alone. They talk through an event bus and a capability registry rather than by importing each other, so a fourth module bolts on without reopening the first three.

Reply engineleasing-reply-engine130 tests

Classifies an inbound message, extracts what the renter told you, plans a reply as speech acts, then writes it in a voice profile learned from the operator's own messages. Guardrails run on the finished text — steering language, protected-characteristic questions, overpromises and robotic tells are all blocked before anything sends.

Lead ingestorlead-ingestor103 tests

Pulls conversations from the Instagram export, a logged-in account, or a dedicated phone on a server, then routes each thread to a disposition with a defined next step. It routes; it does not rank. Industry contacts are separated from renters before triage ever sees them, so a property manager's DM never lands in the renter funnel.

Property directoryproperty-directory92 tests

The rolodex. A parent/child tree of management companies, regions and properties, with dated contact assignments so turnover never rewrites history. Imports an existing CRM export, dry-runnable, and dates records from the file rather than the clock.

The churn problem, and the two-card fix

Why the rolodex carries two cards per property

A leasing agent is at one property for eight months and then at another. Any contact list for this industry rots at the speed of its fastest-rotting entry, which is why every locator's contact list is mostly wrong.

So each property carries two cards on different clocks.

Local card · volatile
The site team

Leasing office, property manager, leasing staff. Displayed as primary, because it is who you actually call. Assumed stale after six months, because it is.

Master card · durable
Inherited from above

The regional who covers this property, and corporate behind them. Maintained once for a whole portfolio rather than once per property — and still valid on the day the entire local card goes bad.

The master card deliberately resolves to the nearest ancestor that has contacts, not to corporate. At a company managing a hundred thousand units nobody calls the VP about one property; the regional covers a handful and actually knows the asset. Proximity beats authority, so the card leads with the person who can answer.

That closes the loop. When the local card goes cold, the console does not just flag it — it names who to ask: ask Nia Patel, regional for North Texas, who is on site now. The answer to churn is not keeping the local card fresh forever, which is impossible. It is always retaining a durable route back to a fresh one.

Nothing to keep up to date by hand Verification is traffic-driven. A reply to a DM, a delivered email, a bounce, a name present in a CRM import — each is evidence, recorded automatically. Asking an operator to press a “still correct?” button is a feature that never gets used.

Commission data

Where the agreed rate per property actually comes from

The open question was whether a central database of broker commission exists. It does not. Commission is a private contract term between a property and a broker, and it moves week to week with concessions. What exists is three partial sources, and one of them is instructive.

SourceWhat it isCatch
NAAL directoryProperties post their own commission terms, registration rules and invoice procedures. Free both ways, nationally uniform.Opt-in, so coverage is only who bothered.
ALNNationwide per-property data from two research call centres running a rotating survey cycle.Subscription. It is a call floor, not a feed.
SmartA weekly survey portal the property fills in itself, in exchange for a comp report — plus locator invoicing, so they see what was actually paid.Subscription, and the model is the product.

Neither vendor solved “collect commission from a hundred thousand properties.” One built a call centre. The other flipped it: the leasing office does the data entry, weekly, for free, because the comp report coming back is worth more than the numbers going in. The moat in both cases is the update loop, not the data — a snapshot is reproducible by anyone.

The decision

The operator already holds a Smart subscription and an existing property inventory, which removes the hard part. Seed from those rather than crawling the open web for a starting set, then poll politely and continuously for the delta — the commission and bonus changes — rather than re-pulling a corpus. Treat it as one source among several, stamped with provenance, never overriding the operator's own records.

Two things to keep in view Automated extraction from a subscription platform is a contract question that lands on the account holder, and a materially larger one if this tool is ever sold — vendors of this kind normally sell an export, and asking is cheaper than the exposure. Separately: posted is not paid. When a lease pays out, the amount received either confirms the recorded rate or contradicts it, and the gap between the two is the fact no directory will ever hold.
